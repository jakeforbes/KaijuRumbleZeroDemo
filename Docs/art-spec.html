01Render constraints

Every dimension elsewhere on this page can be adjusted later. These four are structural — break one and the affected sprites need re-rendering, not just re-importing.

Constraint 1

Fixed frame. Never crop, never trim.

Set the render resolution once per entity and hold it across every frame of every animation. Turn off auto-crop and “trim to content” on export. The ground contact point must land on the same pixel in all frames — that pixel becomes the sprite’s pivot, and the engine sorts and collides against it.

Constraint 2

Ground contact at 50% width, 88% height.

In every frame, the point where the model meets the ground sits horizontally centred, 88% of the way down the frame. The 12% below is headroom for feet splay, dust and overshoot on attack frames. One consistent pivot means it is set project-wide rather than per asset.

Constraint 3

No baked ground shadows.

Do not render a contact shadow into the sprite. The engine draws its own as a separate object so it sorts correctly beneath other things. A baked shadow will sit on top of whatever the character walks over.

Constraint 4

Bake emission into the diffuse.

No light sources ship in the build — hundreds of real-time lights would tank the framerate. Neon, glow and self-illumination get baked straight into the render, and one bloom pass in-engine pushes them back up.

02Camera rig

Set once, save as a scene template, reuse for every asset. Assets rendered through different cameras will not sit on the same ground plane.

30°, not 35.264°. True isometric foreshortens all three axes equally and yields a 1.73:1 tile that lands on half-pixels. At 30°, sin(30°) = 0.5 gives an exact 2:1 diamond that tiles cleanly. This is the StarCraft and Diablo convention and what the engine assumes.

Rotate the model, not the camera. Lock the camera and spin the subject on a turntable for each facing — that guarantees identical lighting and identical ground contact across directions. Moving the camera does not.

03Facings — render five, ship eight

Movement is 8-directional. Only five directions need rendering.

Isometric views mirror cleanly across the vertical screen axis. Render at 0°, 45°, 90°, 135°, 180° — named s, se, e, ne, n — and the engine flips them horizontally to produce sw, w and nw at no cost. That is 37% less rendering for an identical result.

One condition: the model must be left-right symmetric. A scar over one eye, a weapon in one hand, or an asymmetric silhouette will visibly flip when the player turns. If a design needs asymmetry, flag it and that entity gets all eight renders.

Why the kaiju renders at 512 when it starts at 128

The kaiju grows to 4× its starting size across a run. Rather than four sets of art, one sprite is scaled in-engine: 128 px tall at size 1, 512 px at size 4. The camera zooms out as the player grows, so the largest it is ever seen is around 256 px. Rendering at 512 means every scale is a downscale (sharp) rather than an upscale (soft), leaves a 2× margin, and covers a 4K build later without re-rendering.

Same logic applies to enemies: each is rendered at roughly double its maximum on-screen height.

05Relative scale

The single most important thing to get right. Growth is the game’s central mechanic, and it only reads if the size relationships are correct — a size-1 kaiju must feel dwarfed, a size-4 kaiju must feel enormous.

Silhouette check. Every entity has to be identifiable as a flat black shape at 25% zoom, because that is roughly what a player sees in a crowded frame. If a Tank and a Mech share a silhouette, the player cannot tell which threat they are dealing with.

06Buildings & ground

One view each — the camera never rotates, so a building is a single still per damage state. Cheapest art in the game and the most of it on screen.

Three damage states, not five: Pristine, Damaged, Rubble. Rubble is short and walkable, so its frame is roughly a third the height.

Barracks and Laboratory are gameplay, not scenery. A player scanning a dense street needs to spot them in under a second, from silhouette alone, at any zoom. Give each a distinct roof shape or signature feature that no filler block shares. If they read as ordinary buildings, players never learn the mechanic.

Rubble state must read as walkable. Once destroyed, the player and enemies move straight over it — it should look like ground, not like a low obstacle.

One reserved-hue constraint. Pickups and enemy telegraphs need a hue range that no building or environment asset uses, so the player can find them in a crowded neon frame. Warm and saturated is the obvious choice against a cool city, but the specific range is yours — it just has to be exclusive.

08Pipeline

Order matters — step 2 is the one that prevents a re-render.

  1. Model, texture, rig

    Bake emission into the diffuse as you go. Keep the silhouette symmetric so mirroring works.

  2. Build the camera rig once, save as a template

    Orthographic, 45° azimuth, 30° elevation, fixed frame size, ground contact locked at 50% / 88%. Every subsequent asset reuses this exact scene rather than rebuilding it.

  3. Turntable the model in 45° steps

    Five positions: 0, 45, 90, 135, 180. The camera does not move.

  4. Render each animation to a PNG sequence

    12 fps, PNG-24 with straight alpha, no crop, no trim, no per-frame resize.

  5. Name and drop into the shared folder

    Deliver per entity as it completes rather than batching — a kaiju in the build on day one is worth more than a complete set on day two.

food_large.png Direction is one of s se e ne n. Frame numbers are 1-based, zero-padded to four digits. All lower case, underscores only — no spaces, hyphens or capitals. Stills omit the direction and frame number.

One folder per entity, one subfolder per animation. Drop them anywhere shared — Drive, Dropbox, a zip. An importer on the engine side handles slicing, pivots and mirroring automatically, so there is no need to open Unity.

Send the first Grunt walk cycle as soon as it exists, even rough. It validates the whole chain — camera angle, pivot, naming, import — on 6 frames instead of on 510. If anything in this spec is wrong, that is when it should surface.
