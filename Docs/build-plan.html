01Shape of the two days

The honest picture before anyone commits to it. Tuning is a scheduled block, not the time left over — the demo exists to answer whether incremental growth feels good, and that question is answered by playing, not by shipping features.

Day 1 ends with the complete core loop playable in greybox: move, smash, eat, grow, shrink, fight, pick up upgrades. If day 1 lands, the design question is already answerable — day 2 makes it presentable.

02Ground rules

Four working agreements that shape every stage below.

  • The arena builds itself from code. A single bootstrap script spawns the greybox city, the player and the spawners at Play. There is almost no hand-authored scene content, so nothing drifts and nothing has to be re-wired after a change.
  • Tuning lives in one asset the builder never overwrites. Every number in the knobs table is a field on a Tuning asset you can edit in the Inspector, including while the game is running. Rebuilding the arena never costs you a tweak.
  • Debug HUD and cheat keys ship in stage 1, not at the end. Forcing a growth tier or skipping to the boss turns a three-minute test into a five-second one. Over two days that difference is worth more than any feature it displaces.
  • One commit per validated stage. “Go back to the last version that felt right” is always one command away.

03Day 1 — core loop in greybox

No art dependency anywhere in this day. Greybox is coloured rectangles and diamonds generated at runtime, so the artist’s schedule and mine never block each other.

Day 1

8.5 h committed · 0.5 h slack
S12.0 h

Arena, movement, camera

Build
  • Clear the Unity sample content; project folders and the Tuning asset
  • Bootstrap script generates a greybox city on a 256×128 diamond grid
  • Player movement: WASD 8-way and gamepad analog, with the vertical axis scaled to 0.5 so motion reads as isometric rather than top-down
  • Y-sorting via Transparency Sort Axis, so the player passes behind buildings
  • Camera follow with edge pan, plus the zoom hook growth will drive later
  • Debug HUD and cheat-key framework
Play and answer
Does moving feel good before anything else exists? Is the isometric squash convincing, or does it read as flat top-down? Can you walk behind a building and still tell where you are?
Knobs unlocked
Move speed, acceleration, camera follow lag, edge-pan margin, iso squash ratio.
S21.5 h

Smashing and food

Build
  • Buildings with HP and three damage states; solid until destroyed, rubble walkable
  • Swipe auto-attack firing on cooldown in the facing direction
  • Food drops in three values, with a pickup radius and magnet-in animation
  • Food meter on the HUD — visible from the first playtest, not added later
Play and answer
Is smashing satisfying with no art and no sound? How many hits should a building take — is the current number tedious or trivial? Does food fly in fast enough to feel like reward rather than chore?
Knobs unlocked
Building HP by type, swipe damage, swipe cooldown, swipe range and arc, food values, pickup radius, magnet speed.
S31.5 h

Growth — the feature the demo exists to prove

Build
  • Continuous scale lerp as the food meter fills, with a punctuated snap and screen shake at each tier
  • Five tiers: HP cap raised, full heal on tier-up
  • Stat scaling — damage ×1.25 per tier, move speed ×1.06 per tier, swipe range tracking the sprite
  • Camera zoom at the square root of scale, so you dominate more of the frame without losing situational awareness
  • Cheat keys to force a tier up or down instantly
Play and answer
The important one. Does growing feel like a reward moment by moment, or only at the tier jumps? Is the continuous scaling visible enough to register, or too subtle? At size 4, can you still see enough of the field to play? This is the session worth repeating five times.
Knobs unlocked
Food per tier, scale per tier, continuous-growth fraction, HP per tier, damage and speed scaling per tier, camera zoom exponent, tier-up shake and pause.
S42.0 h

Enemies, damage, squish, shrink

Build
  • Grunt: chases, attacks on contact cooldown, dies, drops food
  • Player HP, damage flash, 0.5 s invulnerability after a hit
  • Squish — walking over a small-enough enemy kills it instantly and drops its food, no input needed
  • Shrink on death: drop a tier, heal to the new cap, 1.5 s invulnerability and a shockwave that clears space
  • Game over at size 1
  • Cheat keys: spawn a swarm, kill all, toggle invulnerability
Play and answer
Is the first squish at size 2 the payoff it should be? Does shrinking read as a setback you can recover from, or a death spiral? Are grunts a threat at size 1 and harmless clutter at size 4 — and is that the right feeling?
Knobs unlocked
Grunt HP, damage, speed, attack cooldown; player invulnerability windows; squish size thresholds; shockwave radius and force.
S51.5 h

Special attack and upgrades

Build
  • Blast — manually triggered, damages in a line, on its own cooldown
  • Upgrade pickups and the stacking system
  • Five upgrades: Brawler, Fleet, Stomp, Beam, Furnace — all percentage multipliers so they keep pace as size scales your base stats
  • HUD showing what you have collected
  • Cheat key: grant a random upgrade
Play and answer
Do upgrades still feel meaningful once size has multiplied your base damage? Is Blast worth aiming, or does auto-swipe make it irrelevant? Does Stomp change how you move — the qualitative upgrades are the ones that should matter most.
Knobs unlocked
Blast damage, range, width, cooldown; per-upgrade multiplier and stack cap; drop rates.

04Day 2 — content, art, tuning

Art arrives during this day rather than before it. Stage 8 is written so sprites can be swapped in as they land, in any order, without blocking anything else.

Day 2

8.0 h committed · 1.0 h slack
S62.0 h

Wave director and the rest of the roster

Build
  • Timeline-driven spawner running the three-minute arc
  • Tank — armour as flat damage subtraction, so auto-swipe alone will not cut it
  • Mech — elite, drops a power-up
  • Barracks spawning grunts until smashed; Laboratory dropping a power-up
  • Cheat key: jump forward 30 s in the timeline
Play and answer
Does the three-minute arc escalate, or does it spike and flatten? Are you reaching size 3 around the 1:15 mark as intended? Does armour make Blast feel necessary rather than optional?
Knobs unlocked
The full wave timeline — spawn times, counts, types; enemy stat block; barracks spawn rate; drop rates.
S71.5 h

Boss and end states

Build
  • Adult Abomination: telegraphed attacks, meaningful HP, arrival beat
  • Win screen with run stats — buildings smashed, food eaten, peak size
  • Game over and restart, fast enough to re-run without reloading
  • Cheat key: spawn the boss immediately
Play and answer
Can a size-4 kaiju beat the boss but a size-3 kaiju struggle? That gap is what makes the growth mechanic matter. Does the fight last long enough to feel like a finale without outstaying it?
Knobs unlocked
Boss HP, damage, attack cadence, telegraph duration, arrival time.
S82.0 h

Art integration

Build
  • Importer script: slices frame sequences, applies the 50% / 88% pivot, generates the three mirrored facings, builds the animation clips
  • Greybox stands in for anything not yet delivered, so partial art is never a blocker
  • Bloom and the engine-side ground shadows
Play and answer
Do the real sprites sort correctly against buildings? Do collision footprints match what the art looks like it is standing on? Is anything unreadable at size 1 or at full zoom-out?
Knobs unlocked
Bloom intensity and threshold, shadow opacity and size, per-entity footprint offsets.
S92.5 h

Tuning pass and juice

Build
  • Hit stop on heavy impacts, screen shake scaled by your size, impact particles, damage numbers
  • Building collapse effect
  • Full balance pass across the three-minute arc, driven by what you find playing it
Play and answer
Play the full run repeatedly and tune. The question is no longer whether anything works — it is whether the growth curve peaks at the right moment and whether the ending lands.
Knobs unlocked
Everything, all at once, with you driving.

07If we fall behind

Decided now, calmly, rather than at 9pm on day two. Cuts run in this order.

Cut first

Two-player co-op

Already outside the committed hours — it lives in day 2 slack. The input layer is written with two slots from stage 1, so adding P2 is cheap if the time exists and costs nothing if it does not. The shared camera is the real work, not the second player.

Cut second

Mech, and upgrades beyond three

Pure content on systems that already exist. Tank covers the armour lesson on its own; three upgrades still make two runs differ. Each is roughly 20 minutes to add back if the clock allows.

Cut third

Boss art, not the boss

If sprites do not arrive in time, the Adult Abomination ships as a large greybox shape. A grey boss still ends the demo; no boss at all means the run has no ending, which is much worse.

Never cut

Stage 9

If the tuning block gets eaten, the demo ships at whatever values I guessed while writing it, and the thing it was built to prove goes unproven. Everything above exists so that this does not happen.

08Definition of done

The demo is finished when a person who has never seen it can do all of this without being told how.

  1. Pick up a controller and move a kaiju through a city without instruction.
  2. Smash buildings, watch food fly in, and see the meter fill.
  3. Visibly grow — continuously, and in four distinct jumps.
  4. Walk over a grunt that was a threat two minutes earlier and feel the difference.
  5. Take a beating, shrink a tier, and fight back rather than quit.
  6. Collect a power-up and notice what changed.
  7. Reach the boss at around three minutes and beat it — or lose and immediately want another run.

Point seven is the real test. If the first thing a player says at the end is “let me try that again,” the vertical slice has done its job.
